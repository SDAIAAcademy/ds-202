The case

Ghilal Food Bank Network asks one question of four sources

"In the third quarter of 2026, how many parcels did an eligible household receive per month in each district, and which districts are below our standard of two?"

The register

40,000 registrations with personal data. Widad Al-Ghunaim.

Branch logs

210,000 handout rows in 14 files. Hazem Al-Sulaim.

Donations API

Every delivery of food to a branch. Talal Al-Wabil.

Open district table

Population and households, 2022. The municipality.

The question is Sumayyah Al-Khudair's, the programmes director. Participants play Rimas Al-Sinani, the junior analyst; Ruqayyah Al-Marzouqi is the data protection officer who approves every extract. Say plainly that the organisation, the city of Madinat Al-Sidr and every record are synthetic, generated with a fixed seed, and that the records are treated as personal data from the first lab all the same: beneficiaries are a vulnerable group.

Step 1 of Lab 1.1. Ask the room for the unit before showing the table; many answer "a parcel" or "a handout". The population row is the one to dwell on: giving every household the same three months of exposure is a decision the analyst proposes and the director confirms, and it is written into the report.

The rule of the network

Eligibility is income per member, so household size decides who is counted

  • Eligible: an active household whose monthly income per member is at most 1,000 SAR
  • Income 6,000 SAR with 5 members: 1,200 per member, not eligible
  • The same income with 7 members: 857 per member, eligible
  • A household with no recorded size cannot be classified at all

Why this matters on Day 3

Household size is blank for 18% of the population of the question. The denominator of the headline depends on a field that is often missing.

The rule is the network's own and is fictional; do not present it as a national rule. The 18% is measured in Lab 3.1 (18.1% of the 35,665 households of the target population). The point for now: a field is "needed" when a step of the answer cannot be computed without it, even when it is personal and often missing.

Session 2

Files, APIs and databases

  • Load files as text whatever their encoding
  • Page through an API and reconcile with its total
  • Query a register in SQL and build a source inventory
02
How data arrive

Files, an API and a database fail in three different ways

Files

Nothing describes them. Encoding, date layout and units are in nobody's head but the sender's. A file can come twice or not at all.

An API

Data come in pages, the service can be busy, and records change while you read. It usually states a total.

A database

Types and keys are declared, but only the ones somebody declared. Your query decides what you see.

One habit covers all three: before reading a value, count what arrived against what the source says it sent.

In the labs the register is an in-memory DuckDB table loaded from the extract file, and the API is a small server started on the participant's own computer or Colab runtime by G.start_api(); nothing leaves the machine. Say so, so nobody looks for a network address.

Recap of FN-104, one step further

Load every column as text first; decide types later, in steps you can count

  • B01 reads as UTF-8. B03 fails at its first Arabic name: it is in Windows-1256
  • B08 opens, but begins with three extra bytes, EF BB BF: a byte order mark that a spreadsheet wrote
  • A loader that guesses types reads 03/07/2026 as March or as July, and does not say which
  • As text, nothing is lost: 14 files, 210,000 rows, every repair still ahead and countable
What goes wrong. The mark becomes part of the first column name, and log_id is "not found" in one file of fourteen.

Demonstrate: G.log_bytes("B03").decode("utf-8") raises UnicodeDecodeError at byte 0xcc; G.log_bytes("B08")[:6] shows the mark. FN-104 taught code pages on one file per encoding; here the encodings are spread over fourteen files and nobody says which is which. The codec utf-8-sig removes the mark. Sources: Python codecs documentation.

Reading an API

Follow next until it is null, and expect a busy answer

records, url = [], "/v1/donations?page=1&per_page=200"
while url:
    page = G.get_json(base + url)   # retries a 503
    records += page["data"]
    total, url = page["total"], page["next"]
  • The address of the next page comes from the answer; do not compute it
  • 503 means "busy, ask again": wait, retry, give up after a few tries
  • Keep the total the service reports: it is its claim about itself

The first request for page 5 answers 503 once; G.get_json waits and asks again, up to three times. Ask what a loop without the retry does: it stops with an error after 800 records, which is the good case. A loop that swallows the error and continues would silently skip a page.

A provenance record

One short record per source, kept in the repository beside the code, changed in the same commit as the extract it describes.

Return to the answers from the opening question of the day. Provenance here is deliberately modest: a file with one record per source, not a lineage system. The idea of a short standard document that travels with a dataset is the one behind "Datasheets for Datasets". Sources: Gebru and others, Datasheets for Datasets.

The record

Six fields, and what a reviewer needs to read in each

FieldA reviewer needs
originthe system, and how the extract was made
ownerthe person to ask, by role
coveragewhich units, which period, and who is not in it
sharing basisthe consent, agreement or approval, and its purpose
refresh datethe day your copy was taken
limitswhat the data cannot be trusted for

The labs add a seventh key, the classification level of the source.

These six are the fields of outcome LO2. The test of each field is whether a stranger could act on it: "Hazem" is not an owner, "Hazem Al-Sulaim, branch operations lead" is. The reviewer checklist of Lab 1.3 checks form (a full phrase, a real date, at least two limits); a trainer checks substance.

On what basis

Holding an extract is not permission to use it for the next question

  • The register extract came under approval DS-2026-041, for service coverage analysis, Q3 2026
  • The approval names a purpose, the people who may see the data, and that identified records go no further
  • A new question next year is a new request to the data protection officer

In the Data Sharing Policy

Among its principles: sharing serves a legitimate purpose, and access is authorised on a need-to-know and least-privilege basis.

The approval number is fictional. The policy is written for government entities; Ghilal is a charity that adopts its principles as its own procedure, and the slide should be presented that way. Sources: Data Sharing Policy (National Data Management Office), the principles "Legitimate Purpose" and "Authorized Access", read in the English text.

The seventh key

Three of the four sources are Restricted; one is Public

SourceLevelBecause
registerRestrictedpersonal data of beneficiaries

The national policy has four levels: Top Secret, Secret, Restricted and Public. Anything joined to a Restricted source is at least Restricted.

FN-106 taught the levels; this is the recap. The last sentence is the practical one: the joined table that carries a district name from the public table is not public. Day 4 returns to which outputs may leave the analysis environment. Sources: Data Classification Policy (National Data Management Office).

Open data has provenance too

The open table has three dates, and only one is the refresh date

2022

The year the rows describe. It belongs in coverage.

15 March 2023

The day it was published. It identifies the release, in origin.

2 October 2026

The day our copy was downloaded: the refresh date.

And the gap between them is a limit. A table about 2022 used in 2026 describes a city that has since grown, unevenly.

The publisher, the "Open Data Portal of Madinat Al-Sidr Municipality", is fictional. A licence that allows free reuse with attribution is its sharing basis. The Open Data Policy lists licensing and being up to date among its principles; a user of open data still has to record which release was used. Sources: Open Data Policy (National Data Management Office).

Lab 1.3, step 3

A record written in a hurry fails eight checks

The draft

origin: "files" · owner: "Hazem" · coverage: "this year" · sharing basis: "internal" · refresh date: "October" · limits: "messy" · classification: "Internal"

Repaired from the source note

One row per handout, 1 January to 30 September 2026, all 14 branches. Copy of 2026-10-03. Four limits, among them: a week can be uploaded twice or be missing, and no file says so.

"Internal" is not one of the four levels. "This year" will be false in January.

G.review_provenance returns the eight complaints: four fields that are not a full phrase, a coverage with no year, a refresh date that is not a date, fewer than two limits, a level that does not exist. The checklist cannot tell whether a limit is true; that is why the lab makes participants repair the record from the note and not from imagination.

Why limits earn their place

A limit written on Day 1 is an explanation on Day 3

  • "The online form and the call script offer sizes 1 to 6 only": why sizes are missing for large households
  • "No size field on the paper form of B03 and B11 before March 2025": why they are missing at two branches
  • "Sizes are reported, not verified": why an audit was needed
  • "A week can be missing, and no file says so": why a district can look under-served

Each line is a planted problem that a named lab finds: Lab 3.1 for the first two and the last, Lab 3.4 for the third. Ask participants to keep the register note open tomorrow and on Day 3. A limit nobody wrote down is rediscovered, at ten times the cost, by whoever analyses the data next.

Session 4

Coverage and sampling frames

  • Compare a register with an outside frame by district
  • Account for rows that match on one side only
  • Flag under-covered districts and word the caveat
04
Three populations

The question is about households in need; the register lists households that came

In needevery low-income household of the city. Nobody has this list.
→
The framea 2022 survey estimate of low-income households, by district
→
The registerhouseholds that registered, by district
Coverage is the register divided by the frame. It says how much of the population of interest the data can speak for.

A sampling frame is the list a study can reach; the gap between the frame and the population is coverage error. Here even the frame is an estimate. The headline will describe eligible registered households; this session measures how far that is from households in need, district by district.

A ratio above 1

Coverage of 118% says the frame is old, not that the register is wrong

  • D03: 1,113 registered households against an estimate of 940
  • The frame describes 2022; the district has grown since
  • The estimate is from a survey, with no published margin of error
  • "Low income" in the survey is not the eligibility rule of the network
What goes wrong. Reading the ratio as a fact about the register: "178 fabricated households". The denominator is the uncertain part.

All three limits were written into the provenance record of the open table in Lab 1.3. Coverage ratios are approximate in every district, not only in the one where the approximation happens to show. Report them to one decimal less than you computed.

A first kind of missing data

Under-coverage is whole households missing, and not at random

  • Missing values: the household is there, a field is blank. Day 3, Sessions 1 to 3
  • Missing units: the household is not in the data at all
  • Households far from a branch register less; they are also the ones served least
  • The headline is therefore too kind to exactly the districts it is meant to find

No repair, only a report

No imputation of a field brings back a household that was never listed. The coverage table goes beside the headline.

This is the first of the scenarios of LO1: the mechanism (absence related to the outcome itself) and the direction of bias (the figure for under-covered districts is too high as a description of households in need). Day 3 gives the vocabulary; today participants meet the thing itself.
